2. What the Service is, and is not

Pocket Approval is a preliminary building due-diligence and financing feasibility tool. It tells you what you should investigate before you spend money investigating it: it flags potential deal-killing problems early so buyers, agents, attorneys and loan officers can decide where to spend time and money. It is not a replacement for formal due diligence or lender underwriting, and despite its name, it does not approve anything.

  • Not a project approval. Nothing in the Service means a building is approved or eligible for financing by Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA, VA or any lender. Only a lender's own review decides that.
  • Not a loan offer or lending decision. Showing that a lender recently financed units in a building does not mean that lender will finance yours.
  • Not legal, financial, tax, inspection or appraisal advice. Confirm everything with your lender, attorney and other professionals before you rely on it.
  • No broker or lender relationship. We don't arrange loans, receive referral fees from lenders, or act as anyone's agent.
  • Flags such as violations, litigation or special assessments are signals to investigate, not conclusions about a building.
  • How findings are classified. Each finding is labeled as one of: a likely guideline issue (it appears to conflict with a published agency requirement); a potential concern (it may or may not affect financing, depending on the circumstances and the lender); or needs documentation (it can't be assessed without further documents). For example, an operating deficit, pending litigation or an insurance gap is not automatically a Fannie Mae eligibility failure; its effect depends on the facts and on the lender's requirements.
  • A negative assessment is not a "no." A building that appears not to meet standard agency requirements (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA or VA) may still be financed by portfolio, non-agency or exception lenders. Never treat a preliminary assessment as proof that no mortgage is available.
  • Findings from documents other users uploaded are shown with their source type, date and verification status. They may be outdated or incomplete, and the source documents themselves stay private.
